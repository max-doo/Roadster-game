# Art direction

The world is an aerospace automotive test program. The warm-white car is the focal object, with a long dark bronze canopy, clean broad surfaces and restrained metallic detail. Graphite and titanium structures establish scale. Cyan communicates adhesion; orange communicates cold-gas pressure and discharge. Neither color is used as unrestricted neon decoration.

The hangar opens with a live three-quarter car and a compact launch rail. Original geometric architecture, measured material roughness, environment reflections, directional light and a contact shadow give the vehicle volume. The exhibition view provides front, side, rear, top and underside inspection.

Ignition uses late-day warm desert light, geological shelves, concrete road spans and research facilities. Inversion uses a graphite industrial hall, metallic track, structural rings and functional cyan edge strips. Skyfall uses elevated structures above a layered blue-gray canyon, distant cloud banks and warm directional highlights. All backgrounds are actual geometry with atmospheric depth; the sky is an original runtime shader.

Barlow Condensed provides the automotive display voice; Barlow carries instructions and controls. Fonts are bundled locally. UI overlays protect readable text while leaving the driving corridor open. Touch controls occupy opposite lower corners; pitch occupies its own vertical region. Failed attempts return directly to a nearby checkpoint, and decoration never delays retry.

Vehicle animation follows actual speed, acceleration, braking, suspension contact, pitch input, suction and thrust. Effects use bounded particles with directional velocity and finite lifetime. They distinguish dust, adhesion, gas discharge, impact and milestone feedback without obscuring the vehicle.

The final visual acceptance is based on screenshots from the running renderer. Reference photography is research material, not a shipping image asset. The current model is an original game interpretation with documented uncertain engineering details.
